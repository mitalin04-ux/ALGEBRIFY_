Module 07

Linear Transformations and Matrices

There is a profound, one-to-one correspondence between linear transformations and matrices. In this module, you will learn how to construct matrix representations with respect to arbitrary bases, compute standard matrices, perform change of basis, and understand matrix similarity ($B = P^{-1} A P$).

Read Lesson Change of Basis Tool Practice Quiz

Learning Objectives

  • Construct the matrix representation $[T]_{B}^{B'}$ of a linear transformation with respect to bases $B$ and $B'$.
  • Derive the Standard Matrix $[T]$ by evaluating the images of canonical basis vectors $\mathbf{e}_i$.
  • Construct change of basis transition matrices $P$ and transform coordinate vectors.
  • Define matrix similarity ($B = P^{-1}AP$) and identify similarity invariants (trace, determinant, rank).
  • Explain the fundamental algebraic isomorphism between $\mathcal{L}(V, W)$ and $M_{m \times n}(F)$.
Section 7.1

Matrix Representation of a Linear Transformation

Let $T: V \to W$ be a linear transformation. Suppose $B = \{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \dots, \mathbf{v}_n\}$ is an ordered basis for domain $V$, and $B' = \{\mathbf{w}_1, \mathbf{w}_2, \dots, \mathbf{w}_m\}$ is an ordered basis for codomain $W$.

Matrix of $T$ Relative to Bases $B$ and $B'$:

To represent $T$ as an $m \times n$ matrix $[T]_{B}^{B'}$, we apply $T$ to each basis vector of $V$ and write the output as a column vector of coordinates with respect to $B'$:

\[ [T]_{B}^{B'} = \begin{bmatrix} [T(\mathbf{v}_1)]_{B'} & [T(\mathbf{v}_2)]_{B'} & \dots & [T(\mathbf{v}_n)]_{B'} \end{bmatrix} \]

The Fundamental Transformation Identity: For any vector $\mathbf{x} \in V$, the coordinates of the image vector $T(\mathbf{x})$ are given by simple matrix multiplication:

\[ [T(\mathbf{x})]_{B'} = [T]_{B}^{B'} \cdot [\mathbf{x}]_B \]

The Standard Matrix in $\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$

When using standard canonical bases $\mathcal{E}_n = \{\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_n\}$ and $\mathcal{E}_m = \{\mathbf{e}_1, \dots, \mathbf{e}_m\}$, the matrix is called the Standard Matrix of $T$, denoted simply by $[T]$:

\[ [T] = \begin{bmatrix} T(\mathbf{e}_1) & T(\mathbf{e}_2) & \dots & T(\mathbf{e}_n) \end{bmatrix} \]
Section 7.2

Standard Geometric Transformation Matrices in $\mathbb{R}^2$

1. Counterclockwise Rotation

\[ R_\theta = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} \]

Rotates every vector counterclockwise by angle $\theta$. Preserves length ($\det = 1$).

2. Non-Uniform Scaling

\[ S = \begin{bmatrix} k_x & 0 \\ 0 & k_y \end{bmatrix} \]

Stretches or shrinks coordinates by factors $k_x$ and $k_y$.

3. Reflection across X-Axis

\[ M_x = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Flips the sign of the $y$-coordinate while keeping the $x$-coordinate.

4. Reflection across $y = x$

\[ M_{y=x} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \]

Swaps the $x$ and $y$ coordinates: $\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} \mapsto \begin{bmatrix} y \\ x \end{bmatrix}$.

5. Horizontal Shear

\[ H_k = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & k \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Slants the horizontal coordinate: $x' = x + ky, y' = y$. Preserves area ($\det = 1$).

6. Projection onto X-Axis

\[ P_x = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \]

Drops perpendicular onto the $x$-axis, setting $y=0$ ($P_x^2 = P_x$).

Section 7.3

Change of Basis and Matrix Similarity

Theory

The Change of Basis Transition Matrix ($P$)

Let $B = \{\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_n\}$ and $B' = \{\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_n\}$ be two bases for vector space $V$. The transition matrix from $B$ to $B'$ is:

\[ P_{B \to B'} = \begin{bmatrix} [\mathbf{v}_1]_{B'} & [\mathbf{v}_2]_{B'} & \dots & [\mathbf{v}_n]_{B'} \end{bmatrix} \]

To transform coordinate vectors between bases:

\[ [\mathbf{x}]_{B'} = P_{B \to B'} \cdot [\mathbf{x}]_B \]
Definition of Similar Matrices ($A \sim B$):

Two square matrices $A$ and $B$ of order $n \times n$ are called similar if there exists an invertible matrix $P$ such that:

\[ B = P^{-1} A P \]

Geometric Meaning: Similar matrices represent the exact same linear operator $T: V \to V$ described using two different coordinate bases.

Invariants Under Matrix Similarity

If $A$ and $B$ are similar ($B = P^{-1} A P$), they share identical algebraic properties:

  • Same Determinant: $\det(B) = \det(P^{-1}AP) = \det(P^{-1})\det(A)\det(P) = \det(A)$.
  • Same Trace: $\text{tr}(B) = \text{tr}(A)$.
  • Same Rank and Nullity: $\text{Rank}(B) = \text{Rank}(A)$.
  • Same Characteristic Polynomial & Eigenvalues: $\det(B - \lambda I) = \det(A - \lambda I)$.

Key Points

  • The $j$-th column of standard matrix $[T]$ is the image of standard unit vector $T(\mathbf{e}_j)$.
  • Change of basis matrix $P$ converts coordinate vectors: $[\mathbf{x}]_{B'} = P [\mathbf{x}]_B$.
  • Similar matrices satisfy $B = P^{-1} A P$ and represent the same linear map.
  • Similar matrices always have the exact same determinant, trace, and eigenvalues.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not mix up the order of the change of basis matrix. If $[\mathbf{x}]_{B'} = P[\mathbf{x}]_B$, then $[\mathbf{x}]_B = P^{-1}[\mathbf{x}]_{B'}$.
  • Remember that having the same determinant and trace is necessary for similarity, but not sufficient by itself.
