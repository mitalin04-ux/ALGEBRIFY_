Module 09

Determinants

The determinant is a special scalar value associated with every square matrix. In this module, you will learn how to compute determinants using Laplace cofactor expansion, study their fundamental algebraic properties, solve linear systems via Cramer's Rule, and master the invertibility criteria.

Read Lesson Determinant Calculator Practice Quiz

Learning Objectives

  • Define the determinant of $1 \times 1, 2 \times 2, 3 \times 3,$ and general $n \times n$ square matrices.
  • Compute minors $M_{ij}$, cofactors $C_{ij}$, and execute Laplace cofactor expansions along any row or column.
  • Apply algebraic determinant properties ($\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B)$, transpose, row operations, triangular matrices).
  • State the Invertibility Criterion ($\det(A) \neq 0$) and compute inverses using the adjugate formula.
  • Solve linear systems analytically using Cramer's Rule.
Section 9.1

Definition of Determinants, Minors and Cofactors

The determinant is a scalar quantity defined exclusively for square matrices ($n \times n$). It is denoted by $\det(A)$ or $|A|$.

1. $2 \times 2$ Determinant

\[ \det\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{vmatrix} = ad - bc \]

Multiply principal diagonal entries and subtract the anti-diagonal product.

2. Triangular Matrix Determinant

\[ \begin{vmatrix} a & b & c \\ 0 & d & e \\ 0 & 0 & f \end{vmatrix} = a \cdot d \cdot f \]

For upper, lower triangular, or diagonal matrices, determinant equals the product of diagonal entries.

Minors and Cofactors:
  • Minor ($M_{ij}$): The determinant of the $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ submatrix obtained by deleting the $i$-th row and $j$-th column of $A$.
  • Cofactor ($C_{ij}$): The signed minor: $C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j} M_{ij}$.

Laplace Cofactor Expansion ($n \times n$)

The determinant can be evaluated by expanding along any row $i$ or any column $j$:

\[ \det(A) = \sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij} C_{ij} = \sum_{j=1}^n (-1)^{i+j} a_{ij} M_{ij} \quad (\text{Expansion along row } i) \]
Section 9.2

Properties of Determinants

Worked Example

Evaluating a $3 \times 3$ Determinant via Cofactor Expansion

Evaluate the determinant of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 6 \\ 3 & 4 & 8 \\ 6 & 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$ expanding along Row 3:

Step-by-Step Calculation:

\[ \begin{aligned} \det(A) &= a_{31}C_{31} + a_{32}C_{32} + a_{33}C_{33} \\ &= 6 \cdot (-1)^{3+1} \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 6 \\ 4 & 8 \end{vmatrix} + 0 + 2 \cdot (-1)^{3+3} \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{vmatrix} \\ &= 6 \cdot (16 - 24) + 0 + 2 \cdot (4 - 6) \\ &= 6(-8) + 2(-2) = -48 - 4 = \mathbf{-52} \end{aligned} \]

Fundamental Algebraic Properties of Determinants:

  • 1. Transpose: $\det(A^T) = \det(A)$.
  • 2. Multiplicative Law: $\det(AB) = \det(A) \cdot \det(B)$.
  • 3. Row Swapping: Interchanging two rows multiplies the determinant by $-1$.
  • 4. Scalar Scaling: Multiplying a single row by $k$ scales the determinant by $k$. For an $n \times n$ matrix, $\det(kA) = k^n \det(A)$.
  • 5. Row Addition: Adding a scalar multiple of one row to another ($R_i \leftarrow R_i + k R_j$) leaves the determinant unchanged!
  • 6. Proportional Rows: If any two rows or columns are identical or proportional, $\det(A) = 0$.
Section 9.3

Invertibility, Adjugate Matrix & Cramer's Rule

1. Invertibility Criterion

A square matrix $A$ is invertible (non-singular) if and only if $\det(A) \neq 0$. If $\det(A) = 0$, $A$ is singular and has no inverse.

\[ \det(A^{-1}) = \frac{1}{\det(A)} \]

2. Matrix Inverse Formula

\[ A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det(A)} \text{adj}(A) \]

Where $\text{adj}(A) = C^T$ is the adjugate matrix (the transpose of the cofactor matrix $C = [C_{ij}]$).

3. Cramer's Rule

For an $n \times n$ system $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ with $\det(A) \neq 0$:

\[ x_i = \frac{\det(A_i)}{\det(A)} \]

Where $A_i$ is formed by replacing the $i$-th column of $A$ with constant vector $\mathbf{b}$.

Key Points

  • $\det(A)$ is defined only for square matrices ($n \times n$).
  • $A$ is invertible $\iff \det(A) \neq 0$.
  • $\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B)$.
  • $\det(kA) = k^n \det(A)$ for an $n \times n$ matrix.
  • Row additions ($R_i + kR_j$) do not change the determinant.

Common Mistakes

  • $\det(A + B) \neq \det(A) + \det(B)$ in general! Determinants are not additive over matrices.
  • Do not forget the alternating sign pattern $(-1)^{i+j}$ in Laplace cofactor expansions.
  • Remember that the adjugate is the transpose of the cofactor matrix ($\text{adj}(A) = C^T$), not just $C$.
