Module 08

Inner Product Spaces & Orthogonality

An inner product enriches a vector space with geometric notions of length, distance, angles, and perpendicularity. In this module, you will learn the inner product axioms, prove the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and construct orthonormal bases using the Gram-Schmidt process.

Read Lesson Gram-Schmidt Tool Practice Quiz

Learning Objectives

  • State the 4 formal axioms defining a real and complex Inner Product Space $(V, \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)$.
  • Compute induced norms $\|\mathbf{v}\| = \sqrt{\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{v} \rangle}$ and vector distances.
  • State and apply the Cauchy-Schwarz and Triangle Inequalities.
  • Define orthogonal and orthonormal sets of vectors.
  • Execute the step-by-step Gram-Schmidt process to generate orthonormal bases.
Section 8.1

Definition of an Inner Product Space

An inner product on a real vector space $V$ is a function that associates with each pair of vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in V$ a real number $\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle$ satisfying four axioms for all $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w} \in V$ and every scalar $c \in \mathbb{R}$:

The 4 Inner Product Axioms:
  1. 1. Positivity: $\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{v} \rangle \ge 0$, with $\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = 0 \iff \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$.
  2. 2. Symmetry: $\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = \langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{u} \rangle$.
  3. 3. Additivity in First Argument: $\langle \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w} \rangle = \langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{w} \rangle + \langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w} \rangle$.
  4. 4. Homogeneity in First Argument: $\langle c\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = c \langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle$.

Induced Norm, Distance & Cauchy-Schwarz

  • Induced Norm (Length): $\|\mathbf{v}\| = \sqrt{\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{v} \rangle}$.
  • Distance: $d(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \|\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}\| = \sqrt{\langle \mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v} \rangle}$.
  • Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality: \[ |\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle| \le \|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\| \]
  • Triangle Inequality: $\|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}\| \le \|\mathbf{u}\| + \|\mathbf{v}\|$.
Section 8.2

Orthogonality & Orthonormal Sets

1. Orthogonal Vectors

\[ \langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = 0 \]

Two vectors are orthogonal ($\mathbf{u} \perp \mathbf{v}$) if their inner product equals zero.

2. Pythagorean Theorem

\[ \|\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}\|^2 = \|\mathbf{u}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{v}\|^2 \]

Holds for any pair of mutually perpendicular vectors $\mathbf{u} \perp \mathbf{v}$.

3. Orthonormal Set

\[ \langle \mathbf{u}_i, \mathbf{u}_j \rangle = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } i = j \\ 0 & \text{if } i \neq j \end{cases} \]

A set of mutually perpendicular unit vectors of length 1.

Fundamental Theorem on Orthogonal Sets

Every orthogonal set of non-zero vectors $S = \{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \dots, \mathbf{v}_k\}$ in an inner product space is automatically linearly independent.

Section 8.3

The Gram-Schmidt Orthogonalization Process

The Gram-Schmidt Process is an algorithmic procedure that takes any linearly independent basis $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \dots, \mathbf{v}_k\}$ and constructs an orthogonal basis $\{\mathbf{w}_1, \dots, \mathbf{w}_k\}$ (and subsequent orthonormal basis $\{\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_k\}$) spanning the exact same space.

The Gram-Schmidt Formulas:
\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{w}_1 &= \mathbf{v}_1, \quad &\mathbf{u}_1 = \frac{\mathbf{w}_1}{\|\mathbf{w}_1\|} \\ \mathbf{w}_2 &= \mathbf{v}_2 - \langle \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{u}_1 \rangle \mathbf{u}_1, \quad &\mathbf{u}_2 = \frac{\mathbf{w}_2}{\|\mathbf{w}_2\|} \\ \mathbf{w}_3 &= \mathbf{v}_3 - \langle \mathbf{v}_3, \mathbf{u}_1 \rangle \mathbf{u}_1 - \langle \mathbf{v}_3, \mathbf{u}_2 \rangle \mathbf{u}_2, \quad &\mathbf{u}_3 = \frac{\mathbf{w}_3}{\|\mathbf{w}_3\|} \end{aligned} \]
Worked Example

Orthogonalizing Two Vectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$

Convert the basis $\mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$ into an orthonormal basis.

Step 1: First Unit Vector $\mathbf{u}_1$:

\[ \mathbf{w}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \|\mathbf{w}_1\| = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2} \implies \mathbf{u}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1/\sqrt{2} \\ 1/\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix} \]

Step 2: Second Orthogonal Vector $\mathbf{w}_2$:

\[ \langle \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{u}_1 \rangle = (1)(1/\sqrt{2}) + (3)(1/\sqrt{2}) = \frac{4}{\sqrt{2}} \]
\[ \mathbf{w}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} - \frac{4}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{bmatrix} 1/\sqrt{2} \\ 1/\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \]

Step 3: Normalize $\mathbf{w}_2$:

\[ \|\mathbf{w}_2\| = \sqrt{(-1)^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2} \implies \mathbf{u}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} -1/\sqrt{2} \\ 1/\sqrt{2} \end{bmatrix} \]

Verification: $\langle \mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2 \rangle = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\left(-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = \mathbf{0}$. (Perpendicular!)

Key Points

  • $\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = 0 \iff \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$.
  • Two vectors are orthogonal if $\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = 0$.
  • An orthonormal set has mutually orthogonal unit vectors ($\|\mathbf{u}_i\| = 1$).
  • Gram-Schmidt subtracts parallel projections to leave only perpendicular components.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not forget to normalize each intermediate vector $\mathbf{w}_i$ to obtain unit vectors $\mathbf{u}_i$.
  • When computing projections, ensure you project against the normalized orthonormal vectors $\mathbf{u}_j$.
